How to study

  1. Go in orderEach lesson builds on the previous one and takes about an hour with its demos and questions; its own estimate is at the top of the page. It ends with a bridge to the next lesson.
  2. Predict firstEvery demo comes with "Try it" tasks. Say what will happen — which instruction issues next, which block is evicted, which state the cache line ends in — move the control, and only then open the answer.
  3. Watch the red penThe lessons follow a set of student notes (unofficial, 592 pages), cross-checked against the professor's exam texts and Hennessy & Patterson. Where the notes slip — a wrong cycle in a Tomasulo table, a false "always", a formula with a missing term — a red-pen note quotes the notes, gives the correct statement and the reason.
  4. Practise like the examThe real exam is a closed-book written paper of 1 h 30 min with one hand-written A4 sheet of formulas: three exercises (pipeline and Tomasulo tables, VLIW scheduling, caches, coherence protocols), an open theory question and a block of multiple-choice quizzes. Each lesson ends with questions in exactly those forms, with model answers, and a quiz. Progress is stored in this browser.
